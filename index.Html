How it works: a qubit in state α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ is simulated as a 2-element complex state vector. A Hadamard gate takes |0⟩ to (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2, and a measurement collapses it to 0 or 1 with probability |α|² and |β|² (the Born rule).
Honest caveat: this runs on a classical computer, so the "collapse" is decided by the browser's cryptographically secure generator (crypto.getRandomValues). It is an educational simulation, not true quantum randomness. For genuinely quantum randomness you need physical hardware (e.g. photon-based QRNG services). χ² below 3.84 means the 0/1 balance is consistent with a fair source at the 95% level.
